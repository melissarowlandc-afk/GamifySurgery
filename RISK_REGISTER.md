# Risk Register

Status: Living record.

Last updated: 2026-07-26

| ID | Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation | Status |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| RK-001 | Missing detail is invented and later conflicts with owner intent | Medium | High | Keep open questions explicit; require approval gates | Active |
| RK-002 | Incorrect clinical content reaches players | Medium | Critical | Melissa-only approval, source requirements, immutable releases, refreshable fail-closed withdrawal directives, exact targeting, and correction notices | Mitigated by accepted architecture; clinical review process pending |
| RK-003 | Commercial question-bank material is copied too closely | Medium | High | Original drafting, provenance, source review, copyright checks | Active |
| RK-004 | Wrong tutorial answers cause financial or progression softlock | High without controls | High | Guaranteed first-room funding and operating buffer, basic operational revenue, capped consequences, and worst-case simulation | Structurally mitigated by ADR 0025; exact values require simulation |
| RK-005 | Satisfaction above 90% becomes opaque or luck-gated | Medium | High | Visible components, recoverability, bounded random effects | Active |
| RK-006 | Phone interface is technically complete but unpleasant | High | High | Phone-specific layouts, touch testing, staged panels, sound-off testing | Active |
| RK-007 | Browser suspension contradicts facility-time expectations | High without controls | Medium-High | Accepted automatic hidden-page pause, no hidden catch-up, explicit Resume, visible pause/save status, and cross-browser lifecycle tests | Mitigated by accepted architecture; implementation testing pending |
| RK-008 | Two devices overwrite or corrupt a campaign | Medium | High | Save revisions, one active writer, explicit conflict handling | Active |
| RK-009 | Balance or incompatible content edits unpredictably alter existing campaigns | Medium | High | Permanent balance/core pins, immutable releases, explicit clinical compatibility edges, atomic adoption history, and no in-place edits | Mitigated by accepted architecture |
| RK-010 | Random results change after an update | Medium without controls | High | Permanently pin the complete randomness contract, use independent named streams and stable candidate ordering, persist state/counters, retain old implementations, and run golden replay fixtures | Mitigated by accepted architecture; implementation testing pending |
| RK-011 | Admin interface or service credentials are exposed | Low-Medium | Critical | Separate deployment, MFA, outer access gate, server-side secrets, permission tests | Active |
| RK-012 | Email data is used beyond the stated purpose | Low-Medium | High | Purpose limitation, separation, role controls, retention/deletion policy | Active |
| RK-013 | One person creates several accounts despite email uniqueness | Medium | Low-Medium | Single-use invitations and admin review; avoid claiming certainty | Active |
| RK-014 | Security logs become undeclared analytics | Medium | High | Field allowlist, retention, access control, periodic review | Active |
| RK-015 | Pilot drifts into research without approval | Medium | Critical | Research disabled, no telemetry, explicit new IRB/privacy gate | Active |
| RK-016 | Free hosting/database tier loses valuable pilot data | Medium | High | Manual export while disposable; paid backups only after explicit approval; restore tests | Active |
| RK-017 | Full admin website consumes effort before gameplay is proven | High | Medium-High | Minimal publish workflow in vertical slice; expand iteratively | Active |
| RK-018 | One-concept technical slice is mistaken for a valid learning pilot | Medium | High | Separate technical slice from multi-concept human pilot | Active |
| RK-019 | Independent campaign schedules confuse players with multiple campaigns | Medium | Medium | Clearly identify active campaign and retain each schedule separately | Active |
| RK-020 | Framework/provider lock-in becomes expensive | Medium | High | Pure domain layer, standard PostgreSQL, release bundles, adapter boundaries | Active |
| RK-021 | A player accidentally erases a campaign while trying to inspect restart options | Medium without controls | High | At least two deliberate actions, consequence-specific warning, recoverable read-only archive, atomic replacement, and separate permanent deletion | Mitigated by accepted design |
| RK-022 | An FSRS dependency update silently changes existing due dates or makes saved card state incompatible | Medium without controls | High | Project-owned adapter and card types, exact campaign version pins, immutable review evidence, golden histories, and explicit tested migrations | Mitigated by accepted architecture |
| RK-023 | Same-day remediation becomes repetitive, manufactures profitable patient volume, or rewards intentional wrong answers | Medium without controls | Medium-High | One 30-minute alternate-variant remediation per concept/date, no forced arrival, no repeated clinical XP or quality bonus, interleaving preference, and reward simulation with anti-farming invariants | Structurally mitigated by ADRs 0013 and 0025; exact values require simulation |
| RK-024 | Travel, device settings, daylight-saving changes, or later preference changes alter which reviews count as different mastery dates | Medium without controls | High | Confirm one IANA account timezone, use trusted UTC instants, preserve applied offset and derived date, prohibit retroactive recalculation, and test transitions | Mitigated by accepted design |
| RK-025 | One clinical answer falsely credits or penalizes several concepts without showing which knowledge succeeded or failed | High with multi-concept scoring | High | Exactly one primary concept per scored decision, non-scoring supporting tags, and separate observable answers for additional concepts | Mitigated by accepted architecture |
| RK-026 | Additive clinical releases crowd out required concepts, change eligibility, or create new reward exploits in existing campaigns | Medium without controls | High | Directional compatibility reports, pinned balance and core set, selection and reward simulations, owner-controlled distribution, and supplemental-only new concepts | Mitigated by accepted architecture; validation details pending |
| RK-027 | A later content adoption makes it impossible to reconstruct what a learner actually saw | Medium without controls | Critical | Immutable adoption ledger, retained complete releases, episode-level release and revision freezing, and exact scored-decision references in review evidence | Mitigated by accepted architecture |
| RK-028 | A withdrawn invalid question continues to distort FSRS or mastery after future selection stops | Medium without repair | High | Append-only evidence validity, deterministic replay through the pinned scheduler, preserved pre/post repair states, and fresh valid review | Mitigated by accepted architecture |
| RK-029 | Withdrawing the only valid presentation of a required concept makes a campaign impossible | Low-Medium | High | Audited temporary gate waiver without fabricated mastery or APP automation; approved replacement closes it prospectively without revoking passed gates | Mitigated by accepted architecture |
| RK-030 | A player manipulates browser-computed facility state and the result is mistaken for trustworthy assessment, research, or competition data | Medium if incentives change | Critical | Limit the pilot to private single-player nonresearch use, disclose the integrity boundary, server-enforce protected data and immutable evidence, and require a new RED server-authority decision before higher-integrity uses | Accepted limitation; scope gate active |
| RK-031 | Device speed, frame rate, a long browser stall, or a runtime difference changes deterministic facility outcomes | Medium without fixtures | High | Pure fixed-step rules, integer or fixed-precision state, no frame-based probabilities, bounded stall handling, pinned versions, and golden cross-browser replay fixtures | Mitigated by accepted architecture; exact timing remains open |
| RK-032 | A value is treated as authoritative in both the operational snapshot and a relational record, causing silent disagreement | Medium without ownership rules | High | Document one canonical owner per field, transact snapshot and immutable evidence together, reconcile cached totals, and test migrations and restore paths | Mitigated by accepted architecture; schema details pending |
| RK-033 | Epidemiology or another knowledge-base fact silently becomes a patient-generation probability and creates distorted or stereotyped cases | High without separation | High | Separate descriptive Structured Clinical Facts from explicitly approved scenario value sets and weights; require context, rationale, and clinical approval | Mitigated by ADR 0020; implementation validation pending |
| RK-034 | Independently randomized demographics, anatomy, pregnancy status, findings, vital signs, or labs create an internally inconsistent patient or change the correct answer | High without constraints | Critical | Locked answer-essential facts, finite Clinical Instantiation Profiles, typed slots, declarative dependencies, exhaustive finite-profile and boundary validation, and seeded preview | Mitigated by ADR 0020; implementation validation pending |
| RK-035 | Cosmetic name, age, pronoun, or wording changes are counted as distinct mastery variants or evade repetition controls | High without semantic identity | High | Stable Patient Presentation Variant identity, noncosmetic fingerprint, separate Question Variant identity, and concept-specific exposure evidence | Mitigated by ADR 0020; implementation validation pending |
| RK-036 | Updating a Clinical Topic silently changes or invalidates published questions and prior learning evidence | Medium without revision isolation | Critical | Immutable exact revisions, dependency links, change-impact reports, no inherited live changes, and accepted withdrawal/correction workflow | Mitigated by ADRs 0015, 0016, and 0020; implementation validation pending |
| RK-037 | AI drafting invents citations, copies restricted material, receives PHI, or moves unreviewed clinical content toward publication | Medium without controls | Critical | Exact approved inputs, provenance, no-PHI and copyright warnings, Draft-only outputs, no AI approval/publishing authority, Melissa review, and separate provider/data approval | Authoring boundary accepted in ADR 0020; provider and data handling remain unapproved |
| RK-038 | A facility-stage or capability gate makes an existing FSRS concept impossible to review after the player advances | Medium without coverage validation | High | Earliest-stage semantics, later-stage presentation coverage, capability compatibility checks, and deterministic fallback validation | Mitigated by ADR 0020; implementation validation pending |
| RK-039 | Comprehensive knowledge-base notes, rejected drafts, licensed source text, or AI prompts are unnecessarily shipped to player browsers | Medium without bundle boundary | High | Runtime-only release bundles, permission-separated authoring data, exact dependency references, and export tests | Mitigated by ADR 0020; implementation validation pending |
| RK-040 | Delegated technical autonomy drifts away from the intended player or clinical experience | Medium | High | Keep owner-level game and clinical gates, reconstruct source intent, demonstrate thin playable increments, and record costly technical changes | Mitigated by ADR 0021 and frequent demonstrations |
| RK-041 | Architecture and documentation work delay a playable result until the project loses momentum | High under serial decision gates | High | Prototype-first roadmap, synthetic fixtures, minimum coherent milestones, deferred cloud/admin completeness, and evidence-based iteration | Mitigated by ADRs 0021 and 0022; delivery discipline required |
| RK-042 | Multiple-choice repetition rewards recognition of wording or answer position instead of recall of the clinical concept | Medium without variation | High | Meaningful patient variants, multiple Question Variants, reviewed distractors, safe answer-order shuffling, semantic repetition controls, and explanation review | Mitigated by ADRs 0020 and 0023; content quality remains critical |
| RK-043 | A multi-question patient becomes cognitively exhausting or earlier feedback reveals a later answer | Medium without limits | High | One-to-three scored-node cap, different concept per node, authored ordering, dependency-aware deferred feedback, preview, and validation | Mitigated by ADR 0024; usability testing required |
| RK-044 | Closing, refreshing, or switching charts hides ready clinical work, loses a result, or duplicates an answer, reward, or review | Medium without one lifecycle | High | One persisted encounter workflow state, derived folder and accessible action-required indicators, idempotent result and answer transitions, frozen instances, read-only Resolved reopening, and reload/navigation tests | Mitigated by ADR 0026; implementation testing pending |
| RK-045 | Result waits create boring dead time, misleading ETAs, impossible gates, or little reason to build an in-house service | Medium without tuning | High | Meaningful waits only, transparent facility-time ETA, approved fallback routes, deterministic prototype timing, capability and queue simulation, tutorial activity coverage, and operational-route clinical-invariance tests | Mitigated structurally by ADR 0027; exact values require simulation and playtesting |
| RK-046 | Patience either pressures clinical reading, erases required questions, enables avoidance, or is so forgiving that queues do not matter | Medium without explicit states | High | Only unopened Waiting abandonment, visible generous warnings, Active-case protection, chart-open response grace, capped delay effects, tutorial exemption, no review/XP for unseen departures, and worst-case simulations | Mitigated structurally by ADR 0028; exact values require playtesting |
| RK-047 | Workload capacity deadlocks progression, admits bursts or duplicates, lets routine cases consume protected reserve, or fails to respond to upgrades | Medium without one model | High | Derived occupancy, separate routine limit and protected reserve, one persisted pausable arrival gate, atomic slot release/admission, no eviction on capacity loss, deterministic same-tick order, and worst-case simulations | Mitigated structurally by ADR 0029; exact values require simulation and playtesting |
| RK-048 | Terminal bad outcomes overstate causality, become punitive branches, create extra learning evidence, vary after reload, or bypass management caps | Medium without strict authoring | High | Exact final-choice/profile dispositions, clinical rationale and sources, Melissa approval, deterministic zero-or-one outcome, frozen rendering, correct summary, no extra score, and narrative/economic severity separation | Mitigated structurally by ADR 0030; clinical review and usability testing required |
| RK-049 | A literature-search candidate, title, result rank, publication type, or source count is mistaken for accepted evidence or a clinical conclusion | High without separated queues | Critical | Keep Candidate, Source, Citation, reviewed Contribution, Synthesis Decision, clinical approval, and publication as distinct records; derive Known only from reviewed contributions and labeled clinician opinion | Structurally mitigated by the local Clinical Context Workbench; clinical review remains mandatory |
| RK-050 | Automated scouting silently misses recent literature, repeats stale searches, or hides a failed/partial provider response | Medium | High | Per-gap cadence, literal immutable queries, search-through date, provider totals, partial/failure records, retriable runs, due/stale indicators, and no destructive replacement | Mitigated structurally; live provider behavior requires pilot observation |
| RK-051 | Copyrighted source text, commercial question-bank material, PHI, authenticated links, or provider credentials leak into Git, a player bundle, or an external model | Medium without layered controls | Critical | Ignored private roots, no-PHI acknowledgment, operation-specific default-deny rights, metadata-only scouting, no abstracts/full text, tracked-path and build scans, no external AI in this stage, and clinician-authored original content | Mitigated by local boundaries and tests; source-specific rights review remains required |
| RK-052 | A loopback authoring server exposes private files through Vite filesystem serving, CORS, DNS rebinding, traversal, or an unsafe browser-supplied path | Low-Medium without controls | Critical | Bind only to `127.0.0.1`, strict Host/Origin checks, no CORS, explicit Vite filesystem allowlist, bounded same-origin mutations, no browser file paths, root confinement, and API security tests | Mitigated by Workbench topology; verification required after dependency upgrades |
| RK-053 | A source correction, retraction, or conflicting guideline is flattened into the latest-looking answer and historical rationale becomes unrecoverable | Medium without append-only provenance | Critical | Immutable Source Snapshots and Citations, typed Source Relations, separate supporting/challenging/qualifying contributions, append-only decisions, impact work queues, and no automatic winner | Structurally mitigated; correction-impact automation remains iterative |
| RK-054 | Literature provider automation exceeds service limits or sends a developer identifier/credential into logs or tracked files | Low-Medium | Medium-High | Require ignored local contact configuration, optional ignored NCBI key, serial rate limiting, provider-header backoff, response caching, sanitized errors, no contact value in research records, and free public/polite pools only | Mitigated structurally; contact registration/configuration remains operator responsibility |
| RK-055 | A compressed, malformed, or unexpectedly large PDF/DOCX exhausts the local Workbench or leaks parser details | Medium without isolation | High | Fixed intake ceiling, isolated parser worker, execution deadline, V8 heap/stack limits, page/block/output caps, parent-side validation, generic errors, and trusted-source pilot scope | Substantially mitigated for the local pilot; this is not an operating-system malware sandbox |
| RK-056 | Repeated searches create duplicate publication queues or incorrectly reuse one screening decision across unrelated Evidence Gaps | High over recurring scouts | High | Canonical DOI/PMID/provider identities, immutable per-run Candidate Observations, fail-closed identity conflicts, unique per-gap screening series, and run/observation audit | Structurally mitigated; fuzzy bibliographic matching is deliberately excluded |
| RK-057 | Generic or browser-selected reviewer identity makes evidence decisions unauditable or impersonable | Medium without profiles | High | Stable server-resolved reviewer ID and role, no identity field in browser commands, separate automation actor, immutable timestamps, and no role-based clinical approval implication | Mitigated for single-user/local review; distinct profiles are required before multiple reviewers share a workspace |
