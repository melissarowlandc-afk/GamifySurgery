## Exact approved variants

### `concept.men2a.core-manifestation-pattern`

#### 1A — `presentation.men2a.1a` / `question.men2a.1a.v1`

Presentation: An adult patient with medullary thyroid carcinoma is also found to have a pheochromocytoma and PTH-dependent hypercalcemia.

Stem: Which inherited syndrome best fits this combination?

1. Multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1
2. Multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2A
3. Multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2B

Keyed answer: Multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2A

Approved explanation: Multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2A

#### 1B — `presentation.men2a.1b` / `question.men2a.1b.v1`

Presentation: A patient with confirmed MEN2A is being counseled after medullary thyroid carcinoma is diagnosed.

Stem: Which pair of additional manifestations belongs to the syndrome’s core pattern?

1. Mucosal neuromas and intestinal ganglioneuromatosis
2. Pheochromocytoma and primary hyperparathyroidism
3. Pituitary adenoma and pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor

Keyed answer: Pheochromocytoma and primary hyperparathyroidism

Approved explanation: Pheochromocytoma and primary hyperparathyroidism

#### 1C — `presentation.men2a.1c` / `question.men2a.1c.v1`

Presentation: A patient from a family affected by medullary thyroid carcinoma and pheochromocytoma now has hypercalcemia with an inappropriately elevated PTH level.

Stem: Which MEN phenotype best explains this pattern?

1. Multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2B
2. Multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1
3. Multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2A

Keyed answer: Multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2A

Approved explanation: Multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2A

#### 1D — `presentation.men2a.1d` / `question.men2a.1d.v1`

Presentation: A patient undergoing evaluation for an MEN2A phenotype has pheochromocytoma and primary hyperparathyroidism.

Stem: Which thyroid malignancy completes the characteristic disease pattern?

1. Follicular thyroid carcinoma
2. Medullary thyroid carcinoma
3. Papillary thyroid carcinoma

Keyed answer: Medullary thyroid carcinoma

Approved explanation: Medullary thyroid carcinoma

### `concept.men2a.pheochromocytoma-precedes-thyroid-intervention`

#### 2A — `presentation.men2a.2a` / `question.men2a.2a.v1`

Presentation: A patient with MEN2A and medullary thyroid carcinoma is preparing for thyroid surgery. Testing also confirms a functioning pheochromocytoma.

Stem: Which sequence is safest?

1. Proceed with thyroid surgery before addressing the pheochromocytoma
2. Address the pheochromocytoma before proceeding with thyroid surgery
3. Perform both operations without assigning either condition priority

Keyed answer: Address the pheochromocytoma before proceeding with thyroid surgery

Approved explanation: Address the pheochromocytoma before proceeding with thyroid surgery

#### 2B — `presentation.men2a.2b` / `question.men2a.2b.v1`

Presentation: An asymptomatic patient with hereditary medullary thyroid carcinoma has completed the neck evaluation and is preparing for thyroid intervention.

Stem: Which occult condition must be excluded before proceeding?

1. A functioning pheochromocytoma
2. An insulin-secreting pancreatic tumor
3. A functioning pituitary adenoma

Keyed answer: A functioning pheochromocytoma

Approved explanation: A functioning pheochromocytoma. Absence of classic symptoms does not remove the perioperative concern.

#### 2C — `presentation.men2a.2c` / `question.men2a.2c.v1`

Presentation: A patient with MEN2A has both medullary thyroid carcinoma and a biochemically confirmed pheochromocytoma. The thyroid operation is already scheduled.

Stem: Which modification is most appropriate?

1. Proceed with thyroid surgery because the thyroid malignancy was identified first
2. Perform both operations without establishing a sequence
3. Defer thyroid surgery until the pheochromocytoma is addressed

Keyed answer: Defer thyroid surgery until the pheochromocytoma is addressed

Approved explanation: Defer thyroid surgery until the pheochromocytoma is addressed

#### 2D — `presentation.men2a.2d` / `question.men2a.2d.v1`

Presentation: A patient with MEN2A asks why a confirmed pheochromocytoma changes the order of the planned endocrine operations.

Stem: Which explanation is most accurate?

1. It can compromise interpretation of the thyroid pathology
2. It can precipitate a dangerous perioperative catecholamine crisis
3. It can impair healing of the cervical incision

Keyed answer: It can precipitate a dangerous perioperative catecholamine crisis

Approved explanation: It can precipitate a dangerous perioperative catecholamine crisis

### `concept.pheochromocytoma.alpha-before-beta-blockade`

#### 3A — `presentation.men2a.3a` / `question.men2a.3a.v1`

Presentation: A patient with a functioning pheochromocytoma is being prepared for surgery. Because tachycardia persists, the specialist plans to use both alpha- and beta-adrenergic blockade.

Stem: Which order is appropriate?

1. Establish alpha blockade before adding beta blockade
2. Establish beta blockade before adding alpha blockade
3. Begin alpha and beta blockade simultaneously

Keyed answer: Establish alpha blockade before adding beta blockade

Approved explanation: Establish alpha blockade before adding beta blockade

#### 3B — `presentation.men2a.3b` / `question.men2a.3b.v1`

Presentation: A patient with MEN2A and a functioning pheochromocytoma has a draft medication plan that starts beta blockade before alpha blockade. Both drug classes are expected to be needed.

Stem: Which correction is appropriate?

1. Establish beta blockade, then add alpha blockade
2. Establish both forms of blockade at the same time
3. Establish alpha blockade, then add beta blockade

Keyed answer: Establish alpha blockade, then add beta blockade

Approved explanation: Establish alpha blockade, then add beta blockade

#### 3C — `presentation.men2a.3c` / `question.men2a.3c.v1`

Presentation: A patient with a functioning pheochromocytoma has adequate alpha blockade established, but clinically significant tachycardia persists. The specialist decides that beta blockade is indicated.

Stem: Which next step is consistent with the required sequence?

1. Replace alpha blockade with beta blockade
2. Add beta blockade to the established alpha blockade
3. Delay beta blockade until after tumor resection

Keyed answer: Add beta blockade to the established alpha blockade

Approved explanation: Add beta blockade to the established alpha blockade

#### 3D — `presentation.men2a.3d` / `question.men2a.3d.v1`

Presentation: A patient with a functioning pheochromocytoma inadvertently receives beta blockade before alpha blockade.

Stem: Why can this be dangerous?

1. Unopposed beta-adrenergic stimulation can provoke severe tachycardia
2. Unopposed alpha-adrenergic vasoconstriction can provoke severe hypertension
3. Unopposed dopaminergic vasodilation can provoke severe hypotension

Keyed answer: Unopposed alpha-adrenergic vasoconstriction can provoke severe hypertension

Approved explanation: Unopposed alpha-adrenergic vasoconstriction can provoke severe hypertension
