## Approved clinical scope

The concept applies to a stable adult undergoing ventral or incisional hernia
repair when:

- a small-bowel enterotomy is recognized immediately;
- the enterotomy is securely repaired;
- adequate source control has been achieved;
- contamination is minimal and controlled;
- no ongoing gross enteric spillage exists; and
- the operative team has already determined that a single-stage repair remains
  appropriate.

Within that selected scenario, the approved mesh is permanent macroporous
monofilament synthetic mesh. The patient does not receive biologic mesh,
microporous multifilament mesh, or rapidly absorbable mesh solely because the
field is clean-contaminated.

This is not permission to complete a mesh repair after every enterotomy.
Unstable physiology, uncertain bowel viability, an unrecognized or delayed
enterotomy, incomplete source control, or persistent gross enteric or feculent
spillage falls outside the approved rule.

The decision to continue, convert, or defer repair; mesh plane and fixation;
direct bowel contact; enterotomy repair technique; antibiotics; and management
of uncontrolled contamination remain separate concepts.

## Evidence limitation

Current professional guidance supports permanent macroporous synthetic mesh in
selected complicated ventral or incisional hernia repairs after source
control. Randomized evidence supports permanent synthetic over biologic mesh
in elective clean-contaminated and contaminated single-stage ventral hernia
repair. Enterotomy-specific guidance still emphasizes individualized judgment,
and direct evidence for the exact elective inadvertent-enterotomy scenario is
limited. That limitation is preserved rather than hidden.
