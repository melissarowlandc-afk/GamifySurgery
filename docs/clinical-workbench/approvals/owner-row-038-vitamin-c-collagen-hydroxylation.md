## Approved clinical scope

Vitamin C, or ascorbate, supports prolyl and lysyl hydroxylase activity during
collagen biosynthesis. Inadequate vitamin C impairs proline and lysine
hydroxylation and compromises normal collagen maturation and stability,
contributing to impaired wound healing.

The source row's wording about production of type III collagen is superseded.
The approved biochemical mechanism applies to collagen biosynthesis broadly
and must not be taught as unique to type III collagen.

The biochemical requirement also does not establish routine high-dose
supplementation as a universal treatment for every well-nourished
postoperative patient. This concept prescribes neither a supplement dose nor a
treatment protocol.

### Vitamin identification

`question.wound-healing.vitamin-c.vitamin-identification.v1`

Question:

> Which vitamin serves as a cofactor for hydroxylation of proline and lysine
> residues during collagen biosynthesis?

Approved answer: Vitamin C.

Approved incorrect choices:

- Vitamin A
- Vitamin D
- Vitamin K

### Biochemical step

`question.wound-healing.vitamin-c.biochemical-step.v1`

Question:

> Which step in collagen synthesis depends directly on vitamin C?

Approved answer: hydroxylation of proline and lysine residues in procollagen.

Approved incorrect choices:

- Gamma-carboxylation of glutamate residues in clotting factors
- Conversion of methylmalonyl-CoA to succinyl-CoA
- Increased intestinal calcium and phosphate absorption

### Mechanism explanation

`question.wound-healing.vitamin-c.mechanism-explanation.v1`

Question:

> Which explanation is most accurate?

Approved answer: vitamin C supports the activity of prolyl and lysyl
hydroxylases needed for collagen maturation and stability.

Approved incorrect choices:

- It becomes a structural amino acid within collagen
- It converts type I collagen into type III collagen
- It activates vitamin K-dependent clotting factors

### Mechanism-to-consequence

`question.wound-healing.vitamin-c.mechanism-consequence.v1`

Question:

> Which consequence most directly follows from inadequate vitamin C
> availability during collagen synthesis?

Approved answer: impaired proline and lysine hydroxylation, producing less
stable collagen.

Approved incorrect choices:

- Selective loss of type III collagen with normal type I collagen
- Excessive gamma-carboxylation of coagulation proteins
- Reduced intestinal calcium absorption causing immediate scar mineralization
