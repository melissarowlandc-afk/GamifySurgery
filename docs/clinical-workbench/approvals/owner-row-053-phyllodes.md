## 1. `concept.phyllodes.benign-complete-excision`

1. A patient has a core-biopsy diagnosis of benign phyllodes and is planning local treatment. Which operative plan should be discussed? **Key: Complete excision of the benign phyllodes mass without a prescribed rim or fixed-width margin.** Distractors: complete excision with a fixed 5-mm margin; observation. Explanation: benign complete excision is the approved target, not a fixed 5-mm rule.
2. A patient with benign phyllodes asks about local treatment before surgery. There is no separate concern for residual disease. Which plan is appropriate? **Key: Complete excision of the benign phyllodes mass without a prescribed rim or fixed-width margin.** Distractors: complete excision with a fixed 1-cm margin; observation. Explanation: benign management does not require a fixed 1-cm margin.
3. A patient has a benign phyllodes mass confirmed on pathology review and is choosing a definitive local plan. Which option best fits? **Key: Complete excision of the benign phyllodes mass without a prescribed rim or fixed-width margin.** Distractors: complete excision with a fixed 5-mm margin; complete excision with a fixed 1-cm margin. Explanation: this concept separates complete benign excision from fixed-width margin targets.
4. A patient with a benign phyllodes mass attends a preoperative planning visit. Which counseling statement is correct? **Key: Complete excision of the benign phyllodes mass without a prescribed rim or fixed-width margin.** Distractors: observation; complete excision with a fixed 1-cm margin. Explanation: observation is a plausible alternative to discuss but is not the complete-excision plan for this mass.

## 2. `concept.phyllodes.benign-positive-margin-management`

5. A patient has a benign phyllodes positive margin on final pathology. The review finds no concern for transected or residual tumor. What is the best next discussion? **Key: Observation rather than mandatory re-excision.** Distractors: automatic re-excision; routine axillary staging. Explanation: a positive benign margin alone does not mandate re-excision.
6. A patient with benign phyllodes has ink at one margin after complete excision. No residual lesion is suspected on pathology review. Which plan fits? **Key: Observation rather than mandatory re-excision.** Distractors: create a fixed 5-mm margin; perform sentinel-node staging. Explanation: this is a positive-margin management question, not a margin-target question.
7. Two patients return after benign phyllodes excision with a positive margin. One has no residual concern, while the other has concern that the tumor was transected. Which changed finding supports re-excision consideration? **Key: Concern that the tumor was transected.** Distractors: no residual concern after pathology review; nonsuspicious axillary nodes. Explanation: suspected transection or residual disease is the approved exception.
8. A patient with benign phyllodes and a positive margin has a later imaging review that suggests remaining local disease. Which changed circumstance matters? **Key: Consider re-excision for possible residual disease.** Distractors: assume every positive benign margin requires chemotherapy; add routine sentinel-node staging. Explanation: the trigger is residual-disease concern, not the positive margin alone.

## 3. `concept.phyllodes.borderline-margin-target`

9. A patient is planning complete excision of a borderline phyllodes tumor. What margin target should guide counseling? **Key: Aim for 5 mm.** Distractors: aim for 10 mm; use no margin target. Explanation: the approved borderline planning target is 5 mm.
10. A patient is told final pathology is borderline phyllodes before local planning. Which target applies? **Key: Aim for 5 mm.** Distractors: aim for 1 cm; observe without a margin goal. Explanation: the target belongs to borderline disease.
11. A patient asks whether a 5-mm or 10-mm target applies to the planned borderline excision. Which answer is correct? **Key: Aim for 5 mm.** Distractors: aim for 10 mm; use no stated target. Explanation: this tests target selection only.
12. A patient's pathology is revised from benign to borderline phyllodes before definitive planning. What target should be discussed? **Key: Aim for 5 mm.** Distractors: aim for 10 mm; omit a margin target. Explanation: the classification change alters the planning target.

## 4. `concept.phyllodes.borderline-close-margin-management`

13. A patient returns after borderline phyllodes excision with a 2-mm margin. What management direction is appropriate? **Key: Re-excision.** Distractors: treat the margin as automatically adequate; routine axillary staging. Explanation: a margin under 3 mm is in the approved re-excision category.
14. A patient has borderline phyllodes pathology with a 4-mm margin. What is the appropriate counseling frame? **Key: Individualize the re-excision decision.** Distractors: state re-excision is never appropriate; require axillary dissection. Explanation: 3 to under 5 mm is individualized.
15. A patient returns after borderline phyllodes excision with an exactly 3-mm margin. What counseling frame is appropriate? **Key: Individualize the re-excision decision.** Distractors: state that re-excision is required because the margin is under 3 mm; state that no margin discussion is needed. Explanation: an exactly 3-mm margin enters the individualized 3-to-under-5-mm range.

## 5. `concept.phyllodes.malignant-margin-target`

16. A patient is planning local treatment for malignant phyllodes. What margin aim should guide counseling? **Key: Aim for 10 mm.** Distractors: aim for 5 mm; use no margin target. Explanation: the approved malignant planning target is 10 mm.
17. A patient receives malignant phyllodes pathology after excision planning began. Which target now applies? **Key: Aim for 10 mm.** Distractors: aim for 5 mm; observe without a stated target. Explanation: malignant and borderline targets differ.
18. A patient asks whether the malignant target is 5 mm or 10 mm. Which response is correct? **Key: Aim for 10 mm.** Distractors: aim for 5 mm; omit a target. Explanation: this tests the target only.
19. A patient's pathology is revised from borderline to malignant phyllodes before further planning. What margin aim should be used? **Key: Aim for 10 mm.** Distractors: aim for 5 mm; decide from node examination alone. Explanation: the malignant classification changes the planning target.

## 6. `concept.phyllodes.malignant-close-margin-management`

20. A patient returns after malignant phyllodes excision with a 4-mm margin. What is the recommended direction? **Key: Re-excision.** Distractors: surveillance without a risk/benefit discussion; routine sentinel-node staging. Explanation: a margin under 5 mm is in the re-excision category.
21. A patient has malignant phyllodes pathology with a 7-mm margin. What counseling is appropriate? **Key: Recommend re-excision while discussing surveillance only after explicit risk/benefit review.** Distractors: state surveillance is automatically equivalent; state no further local discussion is needed. Explanation: 5 to under 10 mm has recommended re-excision, with limited discussed surveillance consideration.
22. A patient with malignant phyllodes returns after pathology reports a 7-mm margin. The patient asks how the 10-mm target and close-margin intervals should be understood. Which explanation is correct? **Key: They are consensus management thresholds with evidence limitations, not biological cliffs.** Distractors: they are fixed biological cutoffs that determine recurrence for every patient; they eliminate the need for individualized clinical discussion. Explanation: the target and intervals guide management but retain evidence and consensus limitations.

## 7. `concept.phyllodes.hematogenous-spread-pattern`

23. A patient with malignant phyllodes asks about the usual metastatic route during pathology follow-up. Which explanation is correct? **Key: Predominantly hematogenous spread.** Distractors: predominantly nodal spread; spread only by direct skin extension. Explanation: nodal spread is rare relative to hematogenous spread.
24. A patient reviews malignant phyllodes pathology with no suspicious nodes. Which spread-pattern statement remains appropriate? **Key: Predominantly hematogenous spread.** Distractors: routine regional-nodal spread; a route determined by margin width. Explanation: local margin findings do not define the predominant spread route.
25. A patient asks why routine nodal procedures are not assumed in phyllodes counseling. Which pattern supports that boundary? **Key: Nodal spread is rare relative to hematogenous spread.** Distractors: nodal spread is expected in every case; nodal spread is the only metastatic route. Explanation: this is a pattern statement, not an individual prognosis.
26. A patient with malignant phyllodes has a stable outpatient pathology review after complete local planning. Which route should not be presented as the usual route? **Key: Predominantly nodal spread.** Distractors: predominantly hematogenous spread; rare nodal involvement. Explanation: routine nodal spread is not the usual pattern.

## 8. `concept.phyllodes.no-routine-axillary-staging`

27. A patient with borderline phyllodes has no suspicious axillary nodes on examination or imaging. What axillary plan is appropriate? **Key: No routine sentinel-node or axillary staging.** Distractors: routine sentinel-node biopsy; routine axillary dissection. Explanation: nonsuspicious nodes do not justify routine staging.
28. A patient with benign phyllodes has a stable pathology follow-up and nonsuspicious nodes. Which plan fits? **Key: No routine sentinel-node or axillary staging.** Distractors: sentinel-node biopsy because it is a breast tumor; axillary dissection because pathology is available. Explanation: this boundary applies across phyllodes subgroups when nodes are nonsuspicious.
29. A patient has malignant phyllodes treated with mastectomy, and the axilla remains nonsuspicious on examination and imaging. What axillary plan is appropriate? **Key: No routine sentinel-node or axillary staging.** Distractors: add sentinel-node biopsy because the patient had mastectomy; add axillary dissection because disease is malignant. Explanation: mastectomy and malignant pathology do not create a routine staging indication when the axilla is nonsuspicious.

## 9. `concept.phyllodes.suspicious-node-biopsy-exception`

30. A patient with a history of phyllodes tumor has a suspicious axillary node on follow-up imaging. What is the first axillary diagnostic step? **Key: Image-guided histopathologic needle biopsy.** Distractors: immediate axillary dissection; observation without tissue assessment. Explanation: suspicious nodes are sampled before any dissection discussion.
31. A patient with malignant phyllodes has a newly suspicious axillary node during pathology follow-up. Which action is appropriate first? **Key: Image-guided histopathologic needle biopsy.** Distractors: routine sentinel-node biopsy instead; immediate axillary dissection. Explanation: the exception is driven by a suspicious node, not by malignant pathology alone.
32. A patient with prior borderline phyllodes has a suspicious axillary node and biopsy-proven involvement. After MDT review, what may be considered? **Key: Axillary dissection after MDT discussion.** Distractors: ignore the proven involvement; perform routine sentinel-node biopsy instead. Explanation: dissection is reserved for biopsy-proven involvement after MDT discussion.
33. A patient with a prior benign phyllodes diagnosis has a suspicious axillary node on imaging. Needle biopsy does not show involvement. What is the appropriate next implication? **Key: Do not proceed to axillary dissection on suspicion alone.** Distractors: perform axillary dissection despite the negative biopsy; treat the node as routine sentinel-node staging. Explanation: the dissection exception requires biopsy-proven involvement.
