## Patient and question quality

- One narrow objective and one primary concept per scored node. Preserve stable
  IDs/FSRS; do not redefine an existing card. Version meaningful clinical changes
  under current schema; a genuinely different objective needs its own identity.
- Four substantively varied questions per new concept is the established default:
  vary clinically valid story/context/perspective/distractors, not only names.
  Distinguish wording variants from distinct patient mastery presentations.
- Chief complaints normally 1–5 words, a symptom/question/visit reason. Brief
  presentations generally about 25–45 words and short prompts about 6–14 words
  are guides, not limits that remove necessary clinical facts. Put teaching
  detail in feedback. Avoid cryptic shorthand and unexplained anatomical jargon.
- Present one named fictional patient with explicit age and sex, coherent
  generated name, pronouns and matching existing character. Use approved
  clinical profile constraints; do not hard-code demo identities. Appearance
  is not a substitute for clinical anatomy/pregnancy facts. Race/ethnicity never
  selects disease. No new sprite generation or demographic prevalence weights.
- Separate complaint, presentation and complete question; do not repeat them.
  No abstract "A patient with" openings, detached definition quiz, future-result
  leakage or diagnostic label that gives away the tested answer. Reverse-direction
  questions still describe the same patient, not unrelated patient lineups.
- Single-select, exactly one defensible correct choice. Parallel grammar,
  semantic category, specificity and comparable length; plausible distractors.
  Avoid longest-key cues, explanatory distractors, odd qualifiers and overlapping
  correct answers. Runtime choices shuffle; label any key-first review artifact.
- Explain the key and clinically meaningful distractor boundaries concisely.
  Wrong-answer feedback explicitly identifies the correct answer by text.
  Separate submitted-answer feedback from newly returned findings. Maintain
  patient learning summaries and one-review-per-node FSRS semantics.

## Multi-step clinical flow and facility services

Often use purposeful evaluation -> ordered test/procedure -> actual result ->
interpretation/management/follow-up. Usually 1–3 independently scored nodes;
do not introduce unnecessary testing merely to lengthen a visit. Immediate
emergencies require escalation, not a contrived outpatient test wait. Do not
imply advanced operations occur in a facility lacking their capability.

Connect authored actions to real supported service routes: onsite only with
appropriate room, reachable access, qualified staff, capacity and capability;
otherwise a clinically valid offsite/referral route when the release allows.
Preserve current busy-resource queue behavior, patient travel/returns, bedside
stationary testing, staff reservations, external processing and procedure receipts.
Do not invent a fake onsite capability to make a concept playable. A high-acuity
or intraoperative case may remain deferred even if an offsite test exists.

Use real result gates, hide findings until completion, and present coherent
updates before subsequent questions. Preserve corrected-forward behavior after
incorrect nonfinal answers: wrong answer scored once; do not perform the wrong
test merely because it appeared among choices. Frozen encounters and pending
requests retain their original identities, routes and due times across saves.

Every testing choice INCLUDING distractors, repeat tests, surveillance and
sampling needs neutral runtime estimates via answer-choice-timing.ts/current
central service profiles. Distinguish ordering from interpreting existing results.
Mixed options show the existing No test wait convention; pure diagnosis need
not have timing labels. Never derive wait labels from correctness. Game durations
are editorial placeholders, not medical turnaround claims; do not bake durations
into authored prose. Preserve the future facility-dependent timing design.
