## DVT sources and accepted claims

**CDC-VTE-DIAG-2024:** Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. Testing and
Diagnosis for Venous Thromboembolism. May15,2024.
https://www.cdc.gov/blood-clots/testing-diagnosis/index.html
Government clinical education, original federal factual text; no third-party
images reused. Supports ultrasound as standard DVT imaging and distinguishes
it from invasive venography. Read the actual page, not only a search snippet.

**NHLBI-DVT-2022:** National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute. Venous
Thromboembolism: Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT). Updated September19,2022.
https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/deep-vein-thrombosis
Federal clinical education; original text facts only, excluding the explicitly
copyrighted third-party video. Supports postoperative risk, characteristic
symptoms, compression ultrasound, and outpatient medication treatment in
selected uncomplicated patients.

**ASH-CW-VTE-2014:** American Society of Hematology. Choosing Wisely,
recommendation6, released in the 2014 expansion; live page checked Sept12.
https://www.hematology.org/education/clinicians/guidelines-and-quality-care/choosing-wisely
Professional-society recommendation, public copyrighted material used for
targeted factual verification only. ASH terms allow personal noncommercial
reading and prohibit reproducing protected materials; no open license claimed.
Supports a three-month course for first VTE caused by a resolved major transient
risk such as surgery. Do not generalize to cancer, prior VTE, pregnancy,
persistent risks or minor transient factors.

**NHLBI-VTE-TREAT-2022:** NHLBI. Venous Thromboembolism: Treatment. Updated
September20,2022.
https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/venous-thromboembolism/treatment
Federal clinical education, targeted factual cross-check. Anticoagulation
prevents clot extension/new clot; filters are generally reserved for patients
who cannot receive anticoagulants. Duration depends on provoking factors.

Atomic teaching claims for authoring:

1. A patient with new unilateral swelling/tenderness after major surgery and
   a presentation suspicious for DVT needs prompt venous duplex/compression
   ultrasound. The duplex finding of a noncompressible proximal vein supports
   proximal DVT. Sources CDC-VTE-DIAG-2024 and NHLBI-DVT-2022.
2. A first uncomplicated proximal DVT caused by recent major surgery generally
   receives therapeutic anticoagulation for three months when bleeding risk
   allows and the provoking factor resolves. No routine IVC filter is needed
   when anticoagulation is possible. Sources ASH-CW-VTE-2014 and
   NHLBI-VTE-TREAT-2022. Reassess at completion; no specific drug/dose taught.

Author four original stable postoperative follow-up stories. Explicitly exclude
chest symptoms, hemodynamic instability, threatened limb, active cancer, prior
VTE, ongoing major provoking factors, active bleeding and anticoagulation
contraindications. Arrange prompt imaging; game-time estimates are simulation,
not recommended clinical delay. Use a real initial ultrasound gate. Keep all
initial options as diagnostic studies; a D-dimer-only diagnosis is not the key.
Returned proximal clot results precede management; do not announce DVT upfront.

## ITP sources, update and accepted claims

**PROVAN-ITP-CONSENSUS-2019:** Provan D, Arnold DM, Bussel JB, et al. Updated
international consensus report on the investigation and management of primary
immune thrombocytopenia. Blood Advances. 2019;3(22):3780–3817.
doi:10.1182/bloodadvances.2019000812.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6880896/
Primary international consensus, public copyrighted journal material. Do not
infer an open license from PMC. Targeted readable indexed article text included
the Diagnostic Approach and HIV/HCV Testing sections; direct live PMC requests
returned a browser challenge and the publisher returned403. No article download
or raw corpus stored. Supports routine HIV/HCV testing, clinical exclusion of
other causes and the limitation that no single test confirms primary ITP.

**ASH-ITP-2026:** Cuker A, Terrell DR, Sowdagar H, et al. American Society of
Hematology 2026 guidelines for immune thrombocytopenia (ITP): initial and
second-line therapy in adults with primary ITP. Blood Advances. 2026.
doi:10.1182/bloodadvances.2026021269.
Official guideline landing page:
https://www.hematology.org/education/clinicians/guidelines-and-quality-care/clinical-practice-guidelines/immune-thrombocytopenia-guidelines
Directly verified official 2026 visual summary:
https://www.hematology.org/-/media/hematology/files/clinicians/guidelines/ash-cpg-itp-guidelines-visual-summary-0824.pdf
Professional-society primary guideline summary, public copyrighted source;
targeted medical facts only, no diagram/table/algorithm reproduction. The full
publisher manuscript was inaccessible, so the official summary is the accessed
evidence. It explicitly retains delaying splenectomy for at least one year when
feasible; also emphasizes individualized medical treatment and indications for
surgery. This is a good-practice statement, not a high-certainty trial estimate.

**ASH-ITP-UPDATES-2026:** ASH. Summary of Changes to the 2026 Updated
Recommendations. 2026, version in filename20260827.
https://www.hematology.org/-/media/hematology/files/clinicians/guidelines/ash-itp-summary-of-changes_20260827.pdf
Public copyrighted official update summary, used to check recency. The new
guideline retires several2019 drug-choice recommendations. Do not teach the
old blanket steroid-alone or old pairwise second-line hierarchy. Diagnosis
guidance outside the focused update remains available via the older guideline.

Atomic teaching claims:

3. In an adult with otherwise compatible isolated thrombocytopenia, HIV and
   hepatitis-C testing help identify secondary causes before concluding primary
   ITP. Negative serology alone does not establish the diagnosis; history,
   medication review, counts and smear must also fit. Source
   PROVAN-ITP-CONSENSUS-2019; limitation: one adequate diagnostic consensus source
   used for this precise claim, with indexed full section rather than direct
   publisher access.
4. For persistent ITP of only several months, no severe bleeding and feasible
   medical care, defer elective splenectomy until at least one year after
   diagnosis when possible, allowing potential remission. Source ASH-ITP-2026;
   this is conditional on clinical feasibility and not an absolute prohibition
   on emergency surgery. The2019 guidance agrees on this timing, but the2026
   source governs current teaching. One current guideline is adequate here;
   the two ASH summaries are not independent evidence.

Author patients referred for a surgical opinion four to eight months into
persistent presumed ITP. Their otherwise adequate evaluation lacks viral
serology; arrange it before final planning. They have stable isolated platelet
counts above30×10^9/L, no major bleeding, reliable hematology follow-up and
medical alternatives. Avoid depicting prolonged uninterrupted steroids or an
urgent need for splenectomy. Result gate returns negative HIV/HCV testing;
then ask about elective surgery timing. No unsupported drug superiority,
accessory-spleen objective, prophylaxis schedule or operative treatment in the
clinic. Options must make test ordering explicit so all test choices get times.
