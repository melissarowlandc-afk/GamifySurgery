# Sources for question-writing research — 2026-10-28

Status: bibliographic, rights and claim-mapping register for [the strategy](question-writing-strategy-20261028.md) and [the audit](question-cue-audit-20261028.md). Access/check date for every record below is **2026-10-28**. Clinical claims and illustrative examples remain `needs_clinician_review`; named clinician approval: none.

## Method and rights boundary

This was targeted public-web research plus read-only repository inspection, not a systematic review or an ingestion corpus. Search themes included medical item-writing flaws/testwiseness; pertinent negatives/negative information in vignettes; plausible and functioning distractors/option count; cognitive level/key features; item difficulty/discrimination; retrieval, transfer, repeated exposure and FSRS/game design. Official assessment guidance and openly licensed primary publications were preferred. Searches did not establish an empirical optimum number of negative findings or a validated anti-cue prescription for an FSRS surgical game.

Only metadata, short independently written summaries, claim mappings and links are retained. No external sample question, explanation, table, figure, algorithm, source excerpt or full article is stored in the repository. Worked examples are new drafts from the game's own audited variants. Public access alone is not treated as a reuse license. Rights labels describe the exact artifact inspected; they do not grant product redistribution rights or confer clinical authority.

Creative Commons attribution is supplied through full citations and original links. Summaries are newly written, with no source expression reproduced or adapted. [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/), [CC BY 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/) and [CC BY-NC-ND 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-nd/4.0/) are identified per record. For NC/ND and copyrighted organizational guidance, use is limited here to targeted factual verification, original factual synthesis and citation; no licensed source work is modified or redistributed. Future uses need their own rights review.

Some direct PMC opens returned browser challenges. Public primary-record text and license information returned by the web tool's indexed PMC pages were readable; no challenge, login, paywall or access control was bypassed. Unreadable/paywalled snippets were not evidence. Search hits and bibliographic metadata alone were not treated as clinical verification.

## Assessment and learning sources

### QWS01 — NBME guide

- **Full citation:** National Board of Medical Examiners (NBME). *NBME Item-Writing Guide: Constructing Written Test Questions for the Health Sciences*. 6th ed. October 2024.
- **Organization/authors:** NBME, institutional author. Named current contributors: Melissa S. Billings, Kristine DeRuchie, Steven Go, Kieran Hussie, Allison Kulesher, Jacquelyn Merrell, Amy Morales, Miguel A. Paniagua, Jennifer Sherlock, Kimberly A. Swygert and Julie Tyson.
- **Year; class; access:** 2024; official assessment-organization methodological guide; accessed 2026-10-28.
- **Official URL:** [sixth-edition PDF](https://www.nbme.org/sites/default/files/2021-02/NBME_Item%20Writing%20Guide_R_6.pdf); [official guide landing page](https://www.nbme.org/institutions/nbme-item-writing-guide/). The PDF's cover says October 2024 despite the older directory in its URL.
- **License/reuse:** copyrighted, not CC. The manual conditions copying/distribution on notices, noncommercial educational/scientific use and no modification. [Website terms](https://www.nbme.org/terms-of-use/) also restrict reuse. No document, image, sample item or wording is redistributed here; methodological facts are cross-checked and linked.
- **Authority:** strong direct authority on assessment construction; not a source of patient-management approval.
- **Intended use / claims:** QWE01–03 and QWE09. Targeted chapter 2 focused lead-ins/options; chapter 3 technical flaws including convergence; chapter 4 item/option analysis; chapters 5–6 learner-level/context guidance. No sample questions used as authoring material.
- **Limit:** expert guidance does not establish causal effects for each flaw or an optimal negative count in this game.

- **Organization/authors:** NABP, corporate author; individual authors not stated.
- **Year; class; access:** 2024; public licensing-assessment authoring guidance; accessed 2026-10-28.

NABP's restrictions on the separate subscription products NABPLAW/Survey do not authorize their use; those products were not accessed.
- **Authority:** direct licensing-assessment guidance; not surgical clinical evidence.
- **Intended use / claims:** QWE01, supporting focused application items, a complete task, homogeneous/parallel options and per-option author rationales. Used to cross-check the project standard.
- **Limit:** pharmacy/entry-level context. Its recommendation to avoid patient names is not adopted: project instructions require a specific named patient, age and sex. Its construction rules are not applied as universal mandates to every game context.

- **Limit:** historical guide; not evidence of current MCC exam format, nor justification for introducing multi-select/dangerous-option scoring into this game.

### QWS04 — item flaws and cognitive complexity

- **Full citation:** Rush BR, Rankin DC, White BJ. The impact of item-writing flaws and item complexity on examination item difficulty and discrimination value. *BMC Medical Education*. 2016;16:250. doi:10.1186/s12909-016-0773-3.
- **Organization/journal/authors:** BMC Medical Education; Bonnie R. Rush, David C. Rankin, Brad J. White.
- **Year; class; access:** 2016; peer-reviewed observational item-analysis study; accessed 2026-10-28.
- **DOI/full text:** [publisher article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12909-016-0773-3).
- **License/reuse:** [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) expressly stated in the article; metadata/short original synthesis only. No supplementary example items copied.
- **Authority:** empirical assessment evidence; veterinary cohort, not surgical clinical guidance.
- **Intended use / claims:** QWE02, QWE05–06. Observed association of item complexity/flaws with difficulty and discrimination; caution about implausible extra distractors.
- **Limit:** 1,925 faculty-written veterinary items; observational associations do not isolate every flaw's causal effect or validate resident targets.

### QWS05 — revising flaws, cognitive level and distractors

- **Full citation:** Ali SH, Ruit KG. The Impact of item flaws, testing at low cognitive level, and low distractor functioning on multiple-choice question quality. *Perspectives on Medical Education*. 2015;4:244–251. doi:10.1007/s40037-015-0212-x.
- **Organization/journal/authors:** Perspectives on Medical Education; Syed Haris Ali, Kenneth G. Ruit.
- **Year; class; access:** 2015; peer-reviewed targeted item-revision study; accessed 2026-10-28.
- **DOI/full text:** [publisher article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40037-015-0212-x).
- **License/reuse:** the Springer article states Creative Commons Attribution; the current journal record specifies [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). No article/supplement text reproduced.
- **Authority:** empirical preclinical assessment evidence, not clinical care authority.
- **Intended use / claims:** QWE06, QWE09 and review rationale. Source-specific study screens were p outside 0.40–0.80 and point-biserial below 0.20; these are labeled reference conventions, not universal standards.
- **Limit:** small targeted groups and different cohorts; bundled changes cannot identify a single universally effective revision. Discrimination can improve without a consistent fall in p.

- **Intended use / claims:** QWE04; definition/use of low-selection and adverse option-discrimination flags, plausible common-error alternatives and review of unused choices.
