# Tutorial revamp proposal — the first shift

Research snapshot: October 7, 2026. Sol research/design worker for the Claude Code **GamifySurgery manager**. This is a proposal for review, not an implementation or an approved change in direction. Only this file was written. No Git, installations, web access, browser operation, gameplay changes, or runtime tests were performed. Evidence describes the shared source tree; historical validation below is attributed to its record, not rerun here.

**Recommendation:** replace the acknowledgement tour with a short, playable first shift: **finish one visit → manage one timed visit → build a usable Examination Room → start ordinary play**. Coaching advances when the player does the real thing. Keep both current protected encounters, honest incorrect-answer feedback, the player-built first room, and deliberate level advancement. Put ongoing operational teaching in the planned Alerts & Events guidance system.

The result should be a player who can find actionable work, answer and read feedback, enact a plan, recognize a pending/returning patient, connect a room, and continue independently. Knowing every employee role, service, income source, amenity, or learning-scheduler detail is not a graduation requirement.

## 1. Current-flow inventory

### Reading the inventory

`G` means the coach's **Got It** button: one click that acknowledges/hides a card, usually without performing its highlighted action. `A` means the actual game action; counts begin with the named control visible, exclude scrolling, text entry, travel and waiting, and assume a valid/affordable action. Optional actions and recovery paths are stated explicitly. These are source-derived click counts, not observed playtest measurements.

Every live card except the completion card defaults to `acknowledge-step` / **Got It**. The only additional authored live coach action is **Open Management**. **Turn off tutorials** is available on all non-completion cards. The coach does not disable the highlighted controls or block unrelated play. ### Problems to solve

1. **Acknowledgement is confused with doing.** A highlighted patient/answer/room has its own native action, while the nearby coach asks for Got It. Selecting the exam does not even advance its selection explanation. Most acknowledgements are skippable and memory-only, but three pause-owning ones stop timed care until dismissed. Removing them requires changing behavior, not just reducing words.
2. **The completion story contradicts the chart.** “Dismiss → summary → flip → resolve” is still taught despite the live terminal button filing in one action. Help repeats it. A player who follows the useful native action can skip large coach branches; a test fixture can preserve those branches without demonstrating the real path.
3. **Core time and queue mechanics arrive late.** First arrival and answer cards do not explain that the clock runs while a chart is open. Pause/speed appear at patient two, followed by a tutorial-imposed pause, so the lesson “wait while the clinic runs” immediately becomes “read unrelated cards while the clinic cannot run.”
4. **Short copy is not the same as a short tutorial.** Bodyless first prompts still have a title, joke, action row and opt-out. Other cards stack body + note + flavor + repeated button instructions. The door card teaches placement, removal, a modal and a joke together; Help's Build paragraph lists nearly the whole editor.
5. **The second visit is over-coached instead of testing recall.** Another entrance explanation, another opening explanation, another answer instruction and another feedback/dismiss/file chain repeat the first visit. Coach only needs to intervene at the genuinely new moments: enactment, pending chart, return marker, new result.
6. **Important distinctions are under-taught.** Closing an unanswered chart keeps unfinished work in Existing Patients; closing displayed feedback can enact or file it. Payment is spendable, current-level XP is progression, and satisfaction is separate. A footprint can be valid while the room is inaccessible. None needs a lecture, but each needs one clear action-context sentence.
7. **Too much operational breadth appears before a problem exists.** Emergency consult as a generic waiting pastime, every Management tab, trash, cooler, future automation, deleting doors and disease-reference flipping dilute the first shift. Their useful trigger is need/underuse, not “we reached step N.”
8. **Progress and completion are fragile presentation state.** Most Got Its reset after reload; off/on affects the profile rather than one clinic. The Level-1 prompt applies at all later levels and is not bounded to the first arrival. Disabling guides is not skipping the protected encounters or room requirement, but the UI does not explain the distinction.
9. **Goals can look like a wrong-answer dead end.** Initially the XP/satisfaction route is shown; the safe training route replaces it only once its conditions are satisfied. The generic remaining-goals card tells players to earn XP/raise satisfaction. Make the approved training route explicit in Level-0 explanatory copy, preserving the actual domain eligibility calculation.
10. **The opening fiction and cash disagree.** Four opening actions precede play, and the million-dollar inheritance appears to promise a budget the player does not receive. Keep the dry joke, show the real runtime cash, and let the owner choose the opening edit; do not silently change economy.

These are source-supported design findings. How much they affect abandonment, reading speed, or enjoyment remains unmeasured. Acceptance should include a fresh owner playthrough, not only tests.

## 3. Proposed total revamp

### Goals and structure

Use four milestone labels, with short state-dependent beats underneath: **First visit / Timed visit / First room / Open for business**. Reuse the existing coach's target/highlight and positioning work; replace its acknowledgement interaction model. Show one compact title plus one short instruction, normally no more than about 30 words combined. Integrate the joke into that instruction rather than adding a flavor paragraph to every card. These are editorial targets, not medical or simulation timing claims.

The actual highlighted control remains the action. No Next, Got It, forced “I read this,” required chart flip, tutorial-only final completion click, or duplicate coach copy of the answer explanation. Finish each beat on a successful native action/state transition; attempting an invalid placement is not completion. A player's ahead-of-guide action skips obsolete teaching. If they close a chart, switch modes, pause, or reload, derive the relevant instruction from the encounter/facility state. Do not replay an earlier sequence merely because a bubble was not acknowledged.

Keep the current protected clinical visits and their randomized choices. Coach teaches how to operate the chart, not the answer. Correct and incorrect responses both continue with the current explanation/corrected-plan workflow. Preserve named patients, age/sex display, clinical constraints, stable concept IDs, frozen cases and all review status. This proposal authors only mechanical/UI copy; any later clinical revision belongs to a separate clinician-review lane.

Introduce Pause before the first reading task, but do not require a pointless pause/resume pair. State the rule and let the player use it. If they pause, the active wait/arrival beat points to the real Resume control. Build, Management and Help retain their existing pause ownership. Ordinary guidance tips never capture pause. Chart opening retains its current running-clock behavior.

Give Level-0 Goals a short helper line describing the existing safe route: **“Finish both introductory visits and connect an Examination Room to graduate. Mistakes still count as completed visits.”** Keep honest XP and the canonical eligibility/button; do not make this explanatory line a second progression evaluator. Existing earlier-eligible campaigns can still Advance. Advanced campaigns end the first-shift guide instead of being forced backward to satisfy the script.
